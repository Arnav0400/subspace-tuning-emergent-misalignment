Update Geometry Can Control Emergent Misalignment

Arnav Chavan, Samyak Jain, Kurt Keutzer

TL;DR

  • Fine-tuning on a narrow task can change behavior far beyond the training data. We study whether controlling the directions of weight updates can limit these effects.
  • Subspace tuning (SST) protects leading weight directions. On the 48-question evaluation, conditional EM fell from 8.58% to 4.15% with AdamW and from 7.77% to 2.82% with Muon, at similar observed insecure-code uptake.
  • Update geometry offers a promising way to control fine-tuning. We still need to understand which weight directions support task learning and which affect broader behavior.

Motivation

Exercising control over the domains influenced by fine-tuning is extremely important. Practically, we don’t want the model to demonstrate unexpected behaviour on domains very different from the fine-tuning dataset. Emergent misalignment demonstrates that it is often difficult to exercise precise control: models fine-tuned on bad code become misaligned on multiple harmful domains, generating abusive text, giving out illegal recommendations, etc. [1] In this article, we study whether constraining optimizer updates away from the leading singular directions of the pretrained weights can help limit these changes.

Our working hypothesis is that emergent misalignment can arise from large drifts in shared representations which can potentially change the behaviour of the model on multiple domains. The training task may be narrow, but the representations it changes can participate in behavior across many domains. We suspect that updates which disturb this shared computation can steer the model away from its previously aligned behavior.

The eigenspectrum of the pretrained weights is often observed to be skewed in nature. Their singular directions carry unequal amounts of weight: in the weight used below, the largest 10% of directions account for 49.2% of its squared Frobenius norm. A weight perturbation changes a representation according to how that perturbation acts on the input reaching the layer. The visualizer lets us inspect this sensitivity by changing one spectrum band at a time while holding the input fixed. The horizontal and vertical scales stay fixed throughout.

Based on this, a trivial recipe to update the weights of the model while minimally influencing its existing geometry is by protecting the alignment preserving high norm subspace of the weights. This is essentially what SST [5] proposed to mitigate forgetting. We use SST as a proof of concept and through this blogpost, we want to highlight a research direction where developing fine-tuning recipes over post-trained models requires understanding the connection between the geometry of the trained weight space and its influence on the behavioral performance of language models.

A simple intervention

We use subspace tuning (SST) [5] to study the effect of update geometry on EM.

Wref = UΣV⊤,   PU = I − UkUk⊤,   PV = I − VkVk⊤

ΔWSST = PU ΔWopt PV,   Wt+1 = Wt + ΔWSST

Here, Uk and Vk contain the protected leading singular vectors, ΔWopt is the full optimizer-proposed weight change, and Wref is the initial or periodically refreshed weight matrix.

Results

We follow the model and dataset setting adopted by the EM paper [1]: Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct; 6,000 insecure-code examples trained for one epoch. We use GPT-6.1-Sol as the judge and compare ordinary full-weight fine-tuning with SST under AdamW and Muon. We protect 90% of singular directions across all the weight matrices in the post-trained language model, testing both fixed bases and bases refreshed every 10 updates. We sweep AdamW learning rates: 5e-6, 1e-5, and 2e-5; Muon sweep includes learning rates 5e-4, 1e-3, and 2e-3. Across the sweep, we report the final model with the best ID score across learning rates and basis settings.

We track three key metrics:

  • In-Domain (ID): insecure-code uptake on 100 held-out prompts.
  • Out Of Domain (OOD): prior capability on MMLU-Pro.
  • Emergent Misalignment (EM): free-form questions and broader behavioral benchmarks.

At similar observed ID performance, SST preserves MMLU-Pro capability and reduces several conditional EM measures. Under AdamW, SFT and SST90 reach 92% and 91% insecure ID, respectively, while MMLU-Pro is 52.40% under SFT and 58.17% under SST90. Conditional EM on the original paper’s preregistered questions falls from 8.58% to 4.15% with SST90.

The pattern also appears with Muon: SFT and SST90 both reach 90% insecure ID. MMLU-Pro rises from 53.40% to 57.02%, while conditional preregistered EM falls from 7.77% to 2.82%.

EM rates average each question’s misaligned fraction among coherent scored answers. AdamW SFT and SST90 have 19.52% and 83.06% eligible-answer coverage, with 55 and 116 flagged answers out of 4,800 generations, respectively. Lower conditional EM therefore does not mean fewer flagged outputs overall. Muon’s corresponding eligible-answer coverage is 49.65% and 93.15%, with 118 and 79 flagged answers.

Further evaluations

Following the paper’s Figure 8, we compare “Answer in JSON” and “Python template in question” for the same eight behavioral questions. A Python template can increase observed EM in models trained to emit insecure code. Under AdamW, conditional EM falls from 5.30% under SFT to 0.38% under SST90 using the same Python template. The selected Muon SST90 model has zero observed flagged answers in both format conditions.

This effect varies across behavioral categories. Following Figure 17 from [1], we show Vulnerable user, Illegal advice, and Medical advice from the preregistered set. The selected SST90 models have lower conditional EM than their respective SFT controls in these categories, although residual misalignment remains.

Conclusion

    
These results highlight that protecting the high norm directions of the weights as done by SST can help us exercise more fine-grained control over the fine-tuning process. This is one of the many strategies which can enable us to perform controlled fine-tuning. However, developing more principled methods requires us to understand the geometry of the weight space and its interaction with the behaviour of the model. This is the key research direction we want to draw the attention of the community to!
